Motion and detail

The sensor runs at 30 fps. Preview speed depends on detection, compression and your Wi-Fi.

8 fps

Higher fps makes motion smoother. The preview cannot reach this target if detection or the network is slower.

60 %

Higher quality preserves detail but transfers more data. This changes the JPEG preview only, not the detector input.

Lighting and color

Light and motion

Measuring exposure…

Automatic mode adapts exposure and gain. Manual mode locks them; changing light can make the scene too dark or overexposed.

20 ms

Try 8–12 ms if people look blurred. The camera compensates with gain, so the image may be darker or noisier. This does not directly increase detection fps.

8 ms

Each frame collects light for this duration. Shorter exposure freezes motion; longer exposure brightens the scene but may blur walking people.

Level 9

Amplifies the signal and also increases noise. The level indexes the sensor’s actual gain table; measured gain is shown above in ×.

Image adjustments

These corrections apply before detection. Start with neutral values and preserve detail in shadows and highlights.

0

Brightens or darkens digitally. It cannot recover lost detail or replace good lighting.

100 %

Separates highlights and shadows. Too much contrast hides details the detector needs.

100 %

Color intensity; 0 makes the image grayscale. Keep 100% as a starting point.

Corrects orientation for the preview and detection. Check the entry direction afterward. Changing orientation clears active tracks and preserves totals.

Camera v2.1 has no software-controlled focus motor. White balance remains automatic. Software cannot correct a physically unfocused lens.

Gate and detection

A crossing counts when a person’s center travels from one side of the band to the other. Drag the band or either endpoint over the image.

Place the gate where the body is visible before and after crossing. You can also tap or drag on the image.

12 %

A wider band reduces small position fluctuations but requires more travel before a crossing counts.

The green arrow points toward entry. The opposite direction counts as exit.

5 %

First endpoint of the band. Exclude side areas outside the entrance.

95 %

Second endpoint. Crossings outside these limits do not count.

Excludes boxes spanning almost the full image height and touching both top and bottom edges. Touching one edge is allowed. Reduces close-up torso crossings; always check the framing.

Filter saved.

55 %

Threshold for starting a track. Try 55% first. Tracking can then recover detections of the same person from 30%. Setting this too high misses crossings. It is not a measure of counting accuracy.

Proposals without an identity use dotted boxes. A weak proposal only helps counting if tracking links it to an existing person.

Tracking confirms each side with 2 observations. A person occluded inside the band may resume a crossing up to 900 ms later if the motion fits. Two people sharing one box can cause a missed crossing.

Crossing test

Check calibration at the actual entrance. This test shows changes since you start it; it does not reset device totals.

Test entries—
Test exits—

Start the test and follow the steps below.

  1. Enter once: expect one entry.
  2. Exit once: expect one exit.
  3. Stop inside the band: standing still should not count.
  4. Walk outside its endpoints: this should not count.
  5. Repeat with two people under normal lighting.

This comparison helps check these crossings. It does not certify accuracy in all conditions.
